Press Go to found a population where each circle has two independent halves — left and right — each seeded with a single A mutation among a sea of T/G/C, and watch selection play out, one generation per row.

Each circle has two independent halves — left and right — representing two separate base-pair positions (loci). The founding generation (t=1) seeds exactly one A mutation into the left half's population and, independently, one A mutation into the right half's population; every other half is randomly T, G, or C. Each generation, individuals are randomly paired up (an odd one out sits out the round) and every pair has a litter of 0–7 offspring, drawn from a binomial distribution whose average is density-dependent (boosted well below carrying capacity, suppressed when crowded) and scaled by each parent's fitness — a parent's multiplier is the average of its two halves, with an A half contributing 1 + fitness% instead of 1. Within a litter, each half of each offspring independently inherits that half's letter from one parent by a plain 50/50 coin flip — the two loci assort independently, just like unlinked genes. The run ends when a variant takes over the population on both halves at once — left uniform and right uniform, not necessarily the same letter on each — or when the population collapses to zero.
